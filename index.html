I'm a fourth-year Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Stony Brook University, where I work in the PiCASSo Lab with Prof. Shubham Jain. My research spans wearable health sensing, multimodal physiological time-series modeling, and LLM-based agents for digital health.

I focus on situations where a timely, understandable answer matters. LiftSafe warns lifters about an unsafe repetition before they perform it, using one arm-worn sensor. CareQA helps caregivers of autistic children make sense of their child's sleep and physiological data by asking questions in plain language. With a cohort of World Trade Center 9/11 first responders, I measure sleep for PTSD monitoring using only the phone they already carry.

Before Stony Brook, I earned a B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering from BUET and taught HCI, algorithms, and computer networks as a lecturer at United International University in Dhaka. I received my M.S. from Stony Brook in December 2025 on the way to the Ph.D.

Research

Under submission

IEEE PerCom 2027

CareQA: sensemaking for caregivers in autism care

Caregivers of autistic children, especially children who are minimally speaking, often have to work out on their own what was behind a restless night or a hard day. CareQA lets them ask about it in plain language, such as why was his sleep so restless last night?, and answers from the child's own passively sensed data: wristband physiology, EEG-based sleep staging, an under-mattress sleep sensor, and caregiver diaries from the Simons Sleep Project. CareQA plans each analysis, computes the evidence from the child's record, and explains what the data shows without giving medical advice.

Now: we are building an interactive dashboard that pairs visualizations of a child's sensor data with CareQA's explanations, and designing a caregiver study around it so that caregivers' own questions shape the next version of the system.

Published

IEEE/ACM CHASE 2026

LiftSafe: catching unsafe lifts one repetition early

As fatigue builds during a set, lifting form degrades and the risk of injury rises. LiftSafe predicts whether the next repetition will be safe or unsafe from a single inertial measurement unit (IMU) worn on the upper arm, so a lifter can be warned before it happens. It combines time-series forecasting with contrastive learning and a progression-aware fusion module that tracks fatigue across the set. In a 24-participant study of bench, overhead, and lateral presses with EMG ground truth, LiftSafe reached 87% balanced accuracy under leave-one-subject-out validation.

Ongoing

2025–present

Passive sleep sensing for PTSD

Disrupted sleep is a core symptom of PTSD, but tracking it usually takes a dedicated device or a sleep diary. Working with a World Trade Center 9/11 first-responder cohort, I extract sleep metrics passively from smartphone accelerometer and screen-state data and relate them to clinical PCL scores through longitudinal statistical modeling.
