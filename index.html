I am a fourth-year Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Stony Brook University, advised by Prof. Shubham Jain in the PiCASSo Lab. My research focuses on wearable health sensing, multimodal physiological time-series modeling, and large language model (LLM) based systems for digital health.

My recent work includes LiftSafe, which predicts unsafe strength-training repetitions from a single arm-worn inertial sensor (IEEE/ACM CHASE 2026); CareQA, a multi-agent LLM system that answers caregivers' questions about their autistic child's sleep and physiological data; and passive smartphone-based sleep sensing for PTSD monitoring in a World Trade Center 9/11 first-responder cohort.

I received my M.S. in Computer Science from Stony Brook University in December 2025 and my B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering from Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET). Before joining Stony Brook, I was a Lecturer at United International University in Dhaka, where I taught Human-Computer Interaction, Analysis of Algorithms, and Computer Networks.

Research

Under submission

IEEE PerCom 2027

CareQA: Multi-Agent Sensemaking for Caregivers in Autism Care

CareQA is a multi-agent LLM system that answers caregivers' natural-language questions about their autistic child's sleep, activity, and physiological signals using passively sensed data from the Simons Sleep Project, including wristband physiology, EEG-based sleep staging, an under-mattress sleep sensor, and caregiver diaries. The system plans each analysis, computes the evidence from the child's own record, and explains the findings without giving medical advice.

Current work: an interactive visualization dashboard that pairs a child's sensor data with CareQA's explanations, and a caregiver study that grounds the system's questions and answers in caregivers' needs.

Publication

Published

IEEE/ACM CHASE 2026

LiftSafe: Predicting Unsafe Repetitions in Strength Training

LiftSafe predicts whether the next repetition in a strength-training set will be safe or unsafe using a single inertial measurement unit (IMU) worn on the upper arm, so that a lifter can be warned before the repetition is performed. It combines time-series forecasting with contrastive learning and a progression-aware fusion module that models fatigue across the set. In a 24-participant study of bench, overhead, and lateral presses with EMG ground truth, LiftSafe achieved 87% balanced accuracy under leave-one-subject-out validation.

Ongoing

2025–present

Passive Smartphone Sleep Sensing for PTSD Monitoring

Sleep disturbance is a core symptom of PTSD. In a World Trade Center 9/11 first-responder cohort, I extract sleep metrics passively from smartphone accelerometer and screen-state data and relate them to clinical PCL scores through longitudinal statistical modeling.
